# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Solo players running D&D 5e campaigns through a chat interface. Typically developers or TTRPG enthusiasts at their desk, evening or late night, looking to lose themselves in a story. They're not managing a team or analyzing data; they're inhabiting a character. The context is intimate: one person, one screen, immersed in narrative.

The job to be done: play a compelling, mechanically honest D&D session with an AI dungeon master, where dice rolls matter, choices have consequences, and the world feels real.

## Product Purpose

Tavern is a choose-your-own-adventure game engine built on D&D 5e rules. The LLM narrates; the engine rolls dice, tracks state, and enforces mechanics. The UI is the window into a living story.

Success looks like: a player starts a session intending to play for 10 minutes and looks up an hour later.

## Brand Personality

Warm, Ancient, Immersive.

The voice is a seasoned dungeon master who's been running campaigns for decades. Confident but never condescending. The world is real to them, and they make it real for you. There's weight and texture to everything: torchlight flickers, stone is cold, ale is warm.

The emotional goal is absorption. The interface should disappear. When it works, you forget you're looking at a screen.

## Anti-references

- Generic AI chat interfaces (ChatGPT, character.ai). Plain bubbles, no atmosphere, interchangeable with any chatbot. The UI has zero personality.
- Overwrought fantasy UI (World of Warcraft, heavily themed MMO interfaces). Gold filigree, dragon borders, ornate frames everywhere. Trying too hard. The decoration competes with the content.
- Flat SaaS dashboards. The tool-for-work aesthetic has no place in a game you play for joy.

The sweet spot: enough atmosphere to feel like you're in a world, restrained enough that the narrative text is the star.

## Design Principles

1. **The story is the interface.** Every design choice serves the narrative. If an element doesn't make the story more immersive, it's noise.
2. **Earned atmosphere.** Texture and warmth through considered material choices (type, color, spacing), not through ornamental decoration.
3. **Respect the dice.** Mechanical results (rolls, HP, state changes) are presented with clarity and authority. They're not decoration; they're the rules that make this a game, not fiction.
4. **Quiet when idle, alive when active.** The UI breathes. Dice rolls land with emphasis, forks invite decision, but between actions the interface recedes.
5. **One player, one story.** This is a personal experience. The UI should feel like it was made for you, not for a crowd.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- WCAG AA minimum for all text contrast
- Respect prefers-reduced-motion
- Keyboard navigable (fork selection, command input)
- Color is never the sole indicator of state (rolls use text labels alongside color)
